The DeepNet consistently outperformed BasicNet on ROC-AUC (0.865 vs 0.849) and log loss, suggesting its deeper architecture captures more nuanced survival patterns — though the accuracy gap is small (80.5% vs 79.3%), reflecting the dataset's size ceiling. Interestingly, hyperparameter tuning did not improve either model on the test set, with both tuned variants scoring lower than their baselines; this is a textbook case of overfitting the validation signal when training data is limited to ~710 samples, making the baseline configurations — chosen by domain intuition — more robust in practice. From the error analysis, Pclass 3 passengers produced the highest misclassification rate for both models, which is expected given that third-class survival was driven by a complex mix of factors like deck position and evacuation access that aren't directly captured in the features. Males were misclassified far more often than females, confirming that Sex is the dominant feature but also that the model struggles at the boundary cases — men who survived and women who did not. Finally, examining the false negative distribution revealed that younger passengers were disproportionately predicted to die when they actually survived, likely because the model learned age as a weak positive signal for survival (children first) but not strongly enough to override other correlated features like class and fare.
